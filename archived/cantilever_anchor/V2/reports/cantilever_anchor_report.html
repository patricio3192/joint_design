1. What changes from revision 2, and why

Revision 2 passed every check, but some of its margins were a few millimetres, the pull reached the bars by two parallel welded paths whose split is unknown, and the weakest element was a weld, not a bar. This variant keeps the concept (embed plate, platina behind the top flange, hooked bars, beam top bars as anchor reinforcement) and removes those weak points.

The highest ratios are the beam bars that anchor the breakout body, computed with every placing tolerance against the design at the same time. At nominal positions the same checks give 0.69 or less (section 4). The highest ratios of the steel parts are the platina root at the corner (0.75) and the embed plate over the gap between the platinas (0.67, simply supported, section 3). The VCM has 2 of its 5 top bars in contact under the corner bars; only one bar of each such pair is counted.

2. One load path

  1. Embed plate, through its thickness. Each platina sits right behind the flange, so the pull crosses the 12 mm of plate straight. Over the 40 mm gap between the platinas, the plate bends a little (section 3).
  2. Fillets platina to plate, 8 mm, above and below each platina, along its full width (76 mm). No bar is in the way: the bars start 10 mm behind the toe of these fillets.
  3. Platina, in tension. At the corner the bars sit on one face, 12 mm off its axis, so the root of the platina also bends.
  4. Side welds, 50 mm long on both sides of each bar, ending at the end of the platina.
  5. Bar, from the end of the welds to its hook: 298 mm available, 216 needed.

After the hooks, the same two paths as in revision 2 take the moment out of the joint: the top bars of the concrete beam (path 1) and the pedestal (path 2), step 6.

Edge: 2 bars over and 2 under the platinas, 12 mm above and below the flange line, so their resultant is on the flange line: T = M / (h − tf) = 19.0 / 0.2302 = 82.7 kN
Corner C1 (bars under): T = M / (zbars − zC) = 14.8 / 0.2182 = 67.6 kN (flange 64.1 kN). Corner C2 (bars over): 60.9 kN. Envelope load.

3. Bending of the 12 mm plate: equations and proof

The plate is loaded in four places: the top flange pulls it, the platinas pull it back, the bottom flange pushes it, and the concrete pushes back behind the bottom flange. Each place is checked with a model that ignores any help from the rest of the plate and from the beam web, so every model is a lower bound.

3a. Through the thickness, flange to platinas

Where a platina is behind the flange, the pull crosses the plate as plain tension over the footprint of the flange and its fillets:

h = tf + 2 w = 9.8 + 2 (8) = 25.8 mm;   b = 2 (min(bf/2, v0 + wp) − v0) = 2 (60 − 20) = 80 mm
φRn = 0.9 Fy h b = 0.9 (248)(25.8)(80) = 461 kN ≥ Tflange = 82.7 kN   D/C = 0.18 AISC J4.1(a)

The whole flange force is put on the backed part, although a third of it arrives over the gap. Lamellar tearing is not a concern for a 12 mm plate.

3b. Over the gap between the platinas (40 mm)

The middle 40 mm of the flange has no platina behind it. Its share of the pull goes sideways through the plate to the platinas. Model: a strip of plate as wide as the flange footprint (h = 25.8 mm), spanning the gap, simply supported at the platinas (they are welded on both faces, so the real ends are close to fixed), with the flange share spread uniformly:

F = Tflange (gap / bf) = 82.7 (40 / 120) = 27.6 kN (edge)
M = F L / 8 = 27.6 (40) / 8 = 138 N m
φMp = 0.9 Fy h t²/4 = 0.9 (248)(25.8)(12)²/4 = 207 N m   D/C = 0.67 (0.33 with fixed ends) AISC F11.1: Mn = Fy Z ≤ 1.6 Fy S; Z/S = 1.5
Shear at the ends: F/2 = 13.8 kN ≤ φVn = 1.0 (0.6 Fy) h t = 46.1 kN   D/C = 0.30 AISC J4.2(a)

If the gap share is concentrated at the web instead of spread: a point load at mid-span with fixed ends has the same plastic capacity, 8 Mp/L, so the ratio is the same 0.67. The ends are fixed in reality: the plate is continuous past each platina and each platina is welded on both faces. Only a point load on a simply supported strip would double the ratio, and that combination does not exist here. Nor is the share likely to be more than a third: the flange is very stiff in its own plane and moves load sideways to the backed parts, which are stiffer than the plate over the gap.

The F11 limit for lateral-torsional buckling does not apply: the strip is a plate bent about its weak axis and is held by the flange weld along its length.

3c. The offset of the bars (corner only)

At the corner the bars of each beam sit on one face of the platinas, e = (tp + db)/2 = (12 + 12)/2 = 12 mm off the flange line. That is unavoidable: the bars of X and Y must cross at different levels. The platina hands the plate a moment T e at its root. Following the plate down, the moment falls linearly to zero at the compression flange, where the concrete pushes back:

M = T e = 67.6 (12) = 0.81 kN m   (corner C1, envelope load)
Strip: only the plate under the two platina roots, b = 2 (76) = 152 mm, no spreading: φMp = 0.9 Fy b t²/4 = 0.9 (248)(152)(12)²/4 = 1.22 kN m   D/C = 0.66 AISC F11.1
Shear in the strip: T − Tflange = 3.5 kN, negligible.

Edge. The rows over and under balance, so M = 0. Even if the rows shared the pull 60 / 40 instead of 50 / 50, M = 0.2 T e = 0.20 kN m, D/C 0.16. At ultimate the bars yield and share equally.

The real plate is welded to the beam web along its whole depth and to both flanges, and it spreads the moment wider than the platinas. None of that is counted.

3d. The platina root (corner)

Between the plate and the start of the bars, the platina alone carries the pull of its two bars and the moment of the offset. Per platina, envelope load:

Pr = T/2 = 33.8 kN,   Mr = Pr e = 0.41 kN m;   Pc = 0.9 Fy wp tp = 204 kN,   Mc = 0.9 Fy wp tp²/4 = 0.61 kN m
Pr/Pc = 0.17 < 0.2: Pr/(2Pc) + Mr/Mc = 0.75 ≤ 1.0 AISC (H1-1b)

Beyond the start of the bars the platina and the bars act together, much stronger. At the edge the platina is in plain tension: 0.20.

3e. Bearing behind the compression flange

The plate spreads the push of the bottom flange onto the concrete as a cantilever of length c around the flange footprint, with the plate at its plastic moment (the method of AISC Design Guide 1 for base plates):

fp = φ 0.85 f'c √(A2/A1) = 0.65 (0.85)(20.6)(2) = 22.8 MPa ACI 22.8.3.2
c = √(2 (0.9 Fy t²/4) / fp) = √(2 (0.9)(248)(12)²/4 / 22.8) = 26.6 mm;   A1 = (tf + 2c)(bf + 2c) = (62.9)(173.1) = 10899 mm²
φBn = fp A1 = 248 kN ≥ C = 82.7 kN   D/C = 0.33

√(A2/A1) = 2 needs a concentric area twice as large in each direction: 126 x 346 mm, which fits in the 400 mm column face, centred 235 mm below the beam top. Without the factor 2, D/C = 0.44.

3f. Why 12 mm and not thicker

All plate ratios above are at most 0.67 with models that leave out every helping element. A thicker plate lowers the bending ratios (3b, 3c) with the square of the thickness; the bearing ratio (3e) drops more slowly, because only the spread c grows with t:

But every millimetre of plate pushes the platinas and the bars deeper into the joint, where the clearances are 12 to 18 mm (section 8). 12 mm passes with margin and keeps the layout that was checked.

4. Anchorage without millimetres

The anchorage has two parts. The anchor bars themselves are made in the shop and welded to the platinas, so their hooks are where the drawing says, within a few millimetres. The top bars of the concrete beam, which hold the breakout body, are placed on site, and ACI allows them to be off by:

  • ±25 mm at the ends of bars at discontinuous ends of members ACI 318-19 Table 26.6.2.1(b);
  • ±13 mm in d for members deeper than 200 mm Table 26.6.2.1(a).

Revision 2 used nominal positions; its margin on this check was 27 mm, the size of these tolerances. Here the check is made with all tolerances against the design at once:

  • beam bars 25 mm shorter at the hook and 13 mm lower;
  • anchor hooks 5 mm shorter, platinas 3 mm off level (shop);
  • beam bars at their lowest possible level, since which beam is on top is not known: a VCM under a VCS has its top line at z = -68 and its two extra bars at -80; a VCS under a VCM passes under the stacked corner bars, at -80;
  • the centre bar of each beam stopped behind the mid-face column bar (hook at u = 66 instead of 50).

Step 8. Welds, and which part fails first

A bar lying on a plate leaves a curved groove on each side (flare-bevel). For bars under Ø20, AISC counts only the fillet placed on the groove filled flush AISC Table J2.2, note [a]. So each side weld is rated as a 6 mm fillet. No directional increase (J2-5) is used.

One bar: φRn = 0.75 (0.6 FEXX)(0.707 w)(2 L) = 0.75 (0.6)(483)(0.707)(6)(2 x 50) = 92.2 kN ≥ T/4 = 20.7 kN   D/C = 0.22
Platina to plate, one fillet line: φRn = 153.7 (8)(76) = 93.4 kN. Worst line at the corner: N = T/4 + (T/2) e / tp = 50.7 kN, with V/4 across it: 51.2 kN   D/C = 0.55 (edge 0.24)

Order of failure. If the joint were overloaded, a bar or a platina should yield and stretch before any weld breaks. The welds are checked against the strength of what they connect:

  • side welds of one bar 92.2 kN ≥ 1.25 fy Ab = 59.4 kN (the target of ACI 25.5.7.1 for welded splices): D/C 0.64;
  • bar and platina surfaces along those welds, shear rupture: 108 kN ≥ 59.4: D/C 0.55 AISC J4.2(b);
  • platina fillets at the edge, with both bars at 1.25 fy: D/C 0.64; at the corner the platina root turns plastic first (eccentric pull 53.4 kN per platina, nominal), and the fillets hold it: D/C 0.86. With the expected yield of A36 plate, Ry = 1.3 AISC 341-16 Table A3.1, the fillets still hold at their nominal strength (no φ): D/C 0.84.

Placing the assemblies

From above. Nothing lies above the platinas and the horizontal bars until the closed tie at +45, which goes in afterwards; the platinas pass the mid-face column bar with 12 mm each side and the bars pass the rods of the steel column with 18 mm. The only tight place is where the hook tails come down between the top bars of the beam in line, which cross the whole joint (figure below).

From the front (through the open outer face) is not possible: the hook tails would have to pass through the top bars of the crossing beam and the joint ties. Placing from above is the only way.

Before the pour
  1. Column bars: 90° hooks at the top, turned into the joint (a straight bar has 410 mm where it needs 705 mm; hooked: 259 mm). They are not bent yet: bend the mid-face bars so their hooks run along the steel beam, not across the anchors.
  2. Top bars of the concrete beams: 90° hooks down at the outer face of the joint, tail 12 db = 144 mm, the hook against the outer tie. The centre bar may stop behind the mid-face column bar.
  3. Hold point, measure before the pour (figure in section 4b), from the form of the outer face to the outside of each hook: edge, bars at ±94 at most 115 mm, bars at ±0 at most 126 mm; corner, beam X (type C1) at most 120 mm, beam Y (type C2) at most 105 mm. A hook found farther away is pushed back and re-tied.
  4. VCM: 5 top bars, 3 on the top line (0, ±94) and 2 in contact under the corner bars; the bars under the corners hook down inside the hooks of the corner bars. The anchor tails come down between the beam top bars (Figure in section 8), 12 mm clear.
  5. 4 layers of ties Ø10 in the joint at z = -95 -135 -180 -225 mm, and one closed tie at z = +45 after the assemblies.
  6. Lower the assembly from above; the plate closes the form. The mid-face column bar passes between the platinas (12 mm clear each side; nudge it if needed). Fix the plate to the form and tie the assembly to the cage. Corner: X (type C1) first, then Y (type C2) on top.
  7. Tape or foam over the outer face of the plate so the cement paste does not cover it.
  8. Pour up to the pedestal top (+100). Vibrate well under the platinas.
Before welding the beam (site)
  1. Grind the weld zones of the plate to bright metal, 25 mm beyond each weld, just before welding. If the shop applied a weldable primer, the welding procedure must be qualified with it; grinding is still the safer option.
  2. Weld: fillet 8 all round the flanges, 5 on both sides of the web. Small passes, with pauses, to limit the heat in the concrete around the plate PCI Design Handbook 6.7.4. Prop the beam tip until the slab has hardened.
  3. After welding: brush and paint the plate and the welds with zinc-rich paint.

Shop: bend the hooks before welding (the welds end 250 mm from the start of the bends); bars weldable (INEN 2167 / A706), check the mill certificate for preheat; platinas level with the top flange line within 3 mm; bars start 18 mm behind the plate and are not welded to it.
