Summary
  • Your RAM model cannot be built as drawn (section 2): the centre anchor runs into the mid-face column bar, the second row sits 2.0625 mm over the beam top bars, the top row puts the washer on the flange fillet, and the shear-key pocket cuts the column bar and the ties. RAM also assumes 860 mm of concrete above the top anchors (there are 80) and uncracked concrete.
  • Loads (section 1): from your ETABS cases at the column face, Mu = (1.2 + Ev) MD + ML = 22.08 kN m at the edge (RAM: 18.72). The same factor is applied to the corners.
  • Design (section 3): end plate PL 12x160x350 A36 with a rib PL 12 and 2 extra plates PL 12 on the column side; per beam 2 straight top anchors with a back plate PL 12x45x120 and 2 short shear anchors, all threaded rod 5/8" ASTM A193 B7; grout pad 30 mm; no shear key. Beam IPE 240, welded on site.
  • Every limit state passes. Highest D/C: 0.95 (Top bars at the face: T + M/jd (upper bound, the cantilever counted twice), type CY). Plates proven by strips (lower bound), the strict versions included.
  • Largest moment (section 7): 29.0 kN m (E, C1, CX) and 20.0 kN m (CY), against 22.08 kN m at the edge.
  • Room for site errors (section 9): 6 mm down and 21 mm up in the level of the top anchors at the edge; errors in the plane of the face are absorbed by drilling the end plate to the survey.
  • For you to check on the concrete beam in line (section 13): its 2 bars in contact under the corner bars are a hooked bundle; counted, the beam is at 0.63; not counted, at 1.34.

8. Questions

The end plate is not cast flush

  • Grout pad on a vertical face, 30 mm, non-shrink and flowable, at least 28 MPa; 18 mm under the extra plates (check the minimum of the grout chosen). The face roughened and wetted; the compression passes through it, so a void under the bottom flange is a real defect.
  • Shear anchors through a grout pad: Vsa × 0.80 (17.7.1.2.1), included.
  • The anchors are positioned only by a template during the pour. The end plate is drilled to the surveyed anchors, so errors in the plane of the face disappear; errors in level do not (section 9).

A flush (embedded) plate?

It would act as template and bearing surface (no grout on a vertical face) and remove the 0.80 factor on the shear anchors. The end plate, anchors and back plate keep their dimensions; the anchors stick out 30 mm less. It gives no room in level with a bolted beam; only a beam welded to an embedded plate absorbs level errors of 1 to 2 cm.

Back plate outside, on the far face?

Not better: the rods become through-bolts, outside ACI chapter 17 (17.1.5); the plate would bear on the 40 mm cover outside the ties and stay exposed until the slab is cast. A larger back plate only helps checks that already have room.

Shear key?

Not needed: the shear anchors take Vu (D/C 0.50). Keys beside the centre bar would be limited to about 35 mm deep by the tie layers and the hook tails of the beam bars.

IPE 200? Composite section?

The IPE 200 itself passes, but its shorter lever arm raises the pull on the anchors from 81 to about 97 kN and its narrower flange makes the bearing strip beyond the flange tips fail (about 1.13). A composite section does not help a cantilever: the slab is in tension, and the anchors take the couple at the column face whatever the slab does. IPE 240, not composite.

Rib and extra plates: both needed?

Yes, at E, CX and CY. Without the extra plates the tension side is at 1.29 / 0.68 / 0.99 / 1.11 (E / C1 / CX / CY); without the rib (with the extra plates) at 1.33 / 0.71 / 1.02 / 1.33. C1 would pass without the extra plates; they are used everywhere so that there is one detail.

11. Against the double plate design

With the back plate, this design uses the same holes through the joint, the same anchor reinforcement and the same anchor levels as the double plate. What is left is the face: a flush front plate (template, steel-on-steel bearing; kept flush and vented during the pour) against a grout pad (nothing embedded at the face; grouting on a vertical face, survey and drilling). The double plate's PL 20 end plate is not available either: it would need this same 12 mm plate with rib and extra plates, and its checks redone with the ETABS loads. Rods: B7 there, threaded rod B7 here too.

12. Mistakes found and corrected during this work

  1. DG1 bearing strip beyond the flange tips (n) left out at first: at B = 250 it was 1.60. Plate narrowed to 160.
  2. Yield lines and prying of type CY first computed with the anchor 29 over the flange instead of 56.
  3. A clearance quoted as −1 mm (depth only); the real gap is lateral, 13 mm.
  4. Labels in the drawings read clearances by row number; after rows moved they showed wrong values.
  5. The hook bends of the hooked version hit the top tie at 0; hooks then replaced by the back plate.
  6. Back plate 40 high left 20 mm from the holes to its edges (AISC J3.4M: 22): now 45 high.
  7. Bearing side first checked only with fp without the √(A2/A1) increase; the strict version with the full allowed pressure is now a check (0.79).
  8. The loads of the tributary areas were 12% under ETABS; the design now uses ETABS.

To check in your beam design, or separate the stacked bars at their hooks before the pour. It does not change the connection: the anchors count only the top line.
