Back plate of the top anchors: two ways to stay within the code

ACI 318-19 17.1.5: chapter 17 does not apply to through-bolts or to multiple anchors connected to a single steel plate at the embedded end. The back plate B1 (PL 12x45x120) connects the two top anchors at their embedded end, so the anchor checks of chapter 17 (pullout, blowout, breakout, anchor reinforcement) do not cover it. Both options below are checked for the four types (E, C1, CX, CY), with the ETABS loads (T = 81.3 / 43.0 / 62.1 / 58.5 kN on the two anchors).

A. One plate washer per anchor (stays in chapter 17)

  1. Each anchor gets its own head: nut + plate washer PL 12x45x50 A36, one hole Ø18. The two pieces are 20 mm apart (anchors 70 apart). Holes 25 / 22.5 from the edges (AISC J3.4M: 22).
  2. Effective head: the nut bearing face (dw = 22) plus one plate thickness all round, Ø46, clipped by the plate: Abrg = 1401 mm² (a plate washer counts only as far as it is stiff: the usual limit, from ACI 349 and AISC DG1, is a projection beyond the nut no larger than the plate thickness).
  3. Pullout (17.6.3.2.2a, cracked): φNpn = 0.70 × 8 × 1401 × 20.59 = 162 kN per anchor; D/C 0.25 / 0.13 / 0.19 / 0.18.
  4. Side-face blowout (17.6.4, group of two separate heads, 17.6.4.2): φNsbg = 106.2 / 106.2 / 106.2 / 103.1 kN; D/C 0.77 / 0.40 / 0.58 / 0.57 (was 0.58 / 0.31 / 0.44 / 0.43 with the single plate).
  5. Plate washer bending, cantilever from the nut face: on the effective head p = 29.0 MPa over 12 mm; on the whole plate p = 20.4 MPa over 14 mm. mu = p l²/2 against φmp = 0.9 Fy t²/4 = 8035 N mm/mm; D/C 0.26 / 0.14 / 0.20 / 0.19.
  6. Unchanged: anchor steel, thread stripping, anchor reinforcement (the bearing face stays at u = 364), clearances (the pieces cover the same 120 x 45 as B1, less a 20 mm gap at the middle).
  7. Service: the cone in front of the heads still cracks under D + L (it is edge-limited, see the anchor check page); the beam bars hold it, about 165 MPa at E.
  8. Change on site: two pieces instead of one, each positioned by its own front nut. Nothing else moves.

B. Through-bolts with a plate on the far face (outside chapter 17)

  1. The top anchors run through the column; PL 12x55x120 A36 bears on the far face over a 10 mm mortar bed, holes at mid-height, put on after the forms come off (the bars pass through holes in the far form). One nut and washer behind it; no nut in front. Bars L = 519.
  2. Your idea, in code terms. The plate pushes the top of the column towards the cantilever: a concentrated horizontal load on the top of the pedestal, 1.5 to 56.5 mm above the top of the joint (z = 0). ACI has no "anchor" rule for that, but it does have the member rules for a concentrated load: bearing (22.8), one-way shear of the pedestal (22.5), shear friction across the plane where the load enters the joint (22.9), and, because the load acts within d of its support, the strut-and-tie method (chapter 23).
  3. Bearing on the far face (22.8.3.2): A1 = 6091 mm² (net), √(A2/A1) = 2.00 / 2.00 / 2.00 / 1.71 (the top of the pedestal limits A2); φBn = 139 / 139 / 139 / 118 kN; D/C 0.59 / 0.31 / 0.45 / 0.49.
  4. Plate bending (as section 3b of the anchor page, b = 120, h = 55): between the anchors D/C 0.46 / 0.24 / 0.35 / 0.33; through the holes 0.29 / 0.15 / 0.22 / 0.21.
  5. Pedestal as a cantilever, one-way shear with its ties (22.5): φVn = 192 kN; D/C 0.42 / 0.22 / 0.32 / 0.30.
  6. Where it fails: the load has to cross the top of the joint. Shear friction across z = 0 (22.9, monolithic, μ = 1.4): Avf = T/(φ μ fy) = 184 / 98 / 141 / 133 mm², easily available in the 8 column bars, but 22.9 wants that steel developed on both sides of the plane. Above z = 0 the column bars end in their top hooks, whose outside is only 90 mm (level A) and 53 mm (level B) above the plane, against at least 150 mm for any hook (25.4.3.1). Not developed. The strut-and-tie model says the same: the node at the plate needs a vertical tie anchored above it, and there is no room above it.
  7. Without that steel only plain concrete is left (14.5.5.1): φVn = 0.60 × 0.11 √f'c b h = 48 kN against T = 81.3 kN: D/C 1.70.
  8. Making it work needs vertical bars anchored above the anchors: U-bars over the two through-bolts near the far face, legs developed down into the joint. Their bend would sit at z ≈ 36.9375 to 56.9375, exactly where the level-A column hooks cross the far side (z 74 to 90): the column hooks would have to be redrawn. The steel column rods also cross the plane and are anchored above by the base plate, but only once the steel column is erected, and they carry its uplift: not relied upon.
  9. Service: the shear stress on the plane z = 0 is only about 0.35 MPa under D + L (E), so the top would most likely not crack: the plate spreads the push over the whole far face instead of a small head near the top. That is the real advantage of B, and the reason it needs the U-bars only for the ultimate load.
  10. Also: a mortar bed and a nut on the far face after stripping (the slab covers them later); holes in the far form at the anchor positions; at D4 the two plates are on the west and north faces.

Recommendation

A. It puts the anchors back inside chapter 17 with one change to one piece, and every check passes. B removes the service cracking, but to satisfy the code it needs vertical bars anchored above the anchors, where the column hooks already are; that is a redesign of the top of the column for a crack that the beam bars already control and that ends up hidden under the base plate grout and the slab.

Quoted from memory, to confirm in your copies: the plate-washer stiffness limit (ACI 349 / AISC DG1), μ = 1.4 for monolithic concrete and the development requirement of 22.9, 14.5.5.1 for plain concrete.
