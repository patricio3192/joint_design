Anchorage check: 2 threaded rods 5/8"-11 UNC ASTM A193 B7, back plate PL 12x45x120 A36, the beam bars and the joint

2026-10-02, review page. Type E (edge) step by step; table for the four types at the end. ACI 318-19 chapter 17 (SI), concrete cracked, f'c = 20.59 MPa. Every length used is drawn in the diagram next to it.

Model. The anchors are deformed bars, but their bond is ignored: all of the pull goes to the back plate, which bears on the concrete behind the far column bars like the head of a headed anchor. The concrete in front of it is held by the top bars of the beam in line (anchor reinforcement, ACI 17.5.2.1), because the concrete alone cannot hold it (section 6).

1. Where they are, and the force

  1. Two anchors at |v| = 35 (s = 70), z = +29, from 80 mm outside the face to u = 393.
  2. Back plate bearing face at u = 364: 4 mm behind the far ties (outer face at 360). The end of the bars is 7 mm from the far face (covered by the slab later).
  3. Clear distances: 19 to the front mid-face bar, 13 from the nut corners to the far mid-face bar, 51 to the rods of the steel column.
  4. Pull on the two anchors (end plate page, step 1): T = 81.3 kN; per anchor Ta = 40.6 kN.
  5. Seismic (17.10.5): Ev is 22% of the pull (> 20%); option 17.10.5.3(d) amplifies only Eh, which is zero here. With anchor reinforcement no further reduction applies (17.10.5.5).

2. The bar: steel

  1. Tensile stress area of 5/8"-11 UNC (17.6.1.2): Ase = π/4 (d − 0.9743/n)² = π/4 (15.875 − 0.9743 × 25.4/11)² = 146 mm² (0.226 in²).
  2. futa = min(fu, 1.9 fya, 860) = min(860, 1375.6, 860) = 860 MPa (ASTM A193 B7: fu 125 ksi, fy 105 ksi; elongation 16%, reduction of area 50%: ductile, ACI 2.3).
  3. φNsa = 0.75 Ase futa = 0.75 × 146 × 860 = 94.2 kN (φ = 0.75, ductile steel, Table 17.5.3).
  4. D/C = 40.6 / 94.2 = 0.43
  5. If the end plate holes bore on all four anchors, each top anchor would also take Vu/4 and half the torsion couple: Vua = √[(39.4/4)² + (2.6/2)²] = 9.9 kN; φVsa = 0.80 (grout pad) × 0.65 × 0.6 Ase futa = 39.2 kN.
  6. Interaction (17.8.3): 0.43 + 0.25 = 0.69 ≤ 1.2, D/C 0.57
  7. Thread stripping of the rod inside one nut (14 high): As = π n Le D1,max [1/(2n) + 0.577 (d2,min − D1,max)] = 330 mm², Le = 13; φN = 0.75 (0.6 futa) As = 127.7 kN. Stripping 170 kN > rod rupture Ase futa = 126 kN: the rod breaks first (nut grade 8 or A194 2H) D/C 0.32.
  8. Nuts: hex 5/8"-11 UNC grade 8 (SAE J995) or heavy hex ASTM A194 2H; the nut is stronger than the rod threads. Embedded ends cut flush with the nut (full engagement); at the front, 2 to 3 threads beyond the nut.

3. Back plate: bearing on the concrete (pullout)

  1. Bearing area per anchor: Abrg = (h b − 2 π dh²/4)/2 = (45 × 120 − 2 × π × 18²/4)/2 = 2446 mm².
  2. Pullout (17.6.3.2.2a, cracked, ψc,P = 1): φNpn = 0.70 × 8 Abrg f'c = 0.70 × 8 × 2446 × 20.59 = 282 kN; D/C 0.14.
  3. Where the 8 comes from. Pullout of a headed anchor is the concrete crushing under the head, not a cone. The concrete under a buried head cannot move sideways (it is surrounded by concrete on all sides), so it carries a bearing pressure many times f'c before it crushes: tests on headed anchors give a mean of roughly 12 to 13 f'c in cracked concrete. ACI takes the 5% fractile, 8 f'c (R17.6.3.2.2; Furche 1994, ACI 349). In uncracked concrete ψc,P = 1.4 raises it to 11.2 f'c. Compare: a bearing plate on the surface of the concrete gets only 0.85 f'c, up to 1.7 f'c with confinement (22.8.3.2), because the concrete next to it is free.
  4. Here the pressure under the plate is Ta/Abrg = 40.6/2446 = 16.6 MPa = 0.81 f'c, against 0.70 × 8 f'c = 115.3 MPa.
  5. If only the plate within one thickness (t = 12) of the nut counted as bearing area: Abrg = 1401 mm²; pullout D/C 0.25, side-face blowout (section 4, √Abrg) 0.77. It passes either way; the plate bending checks below show that the plate is strong enough to spread the push over the full area.

3b. Back plate: bending and shear

  1. The plate as a small beam. The concrete pushes on the whole front face of the plate; the two anchors hold it back through their nuts on the back face. So the plate works as a beam 120 long, on two supports (the anchors, 70 apart) with two short overhangs (a = 25). Its cross-section is the plate height times its thickness, 45 × 12, bending through the thickness.
  2. Load: w = T/b = 81285/120 = 677 N/mm (uniform: conservative, the real pressure is higher near the anchors). Each anchor pushes back R = w b/2 = 40.6 kN.
  3. Moment between the anchors (centre, no hole). Half the plate: the nut push R at s/2 = 35 from the centre, the concrete load w b/2 with its resultant at b/4 = 30: M = R s/2 − w (b/2)²/2 = 40.6 × 35 − 677 × 60²/2 = 0.203 kN m. This needs only where the push is centred, not how the nut spreads it, so it is exact.
  4. Equation 5, bending capacity (AISC F11, rectangular bar). Fully plastic, half the thickness yields in tension and half in compression: two blocks of Fy × h × t/2, 6 apart, so Mp = Fy h (t/2)(t/2) = Fy h t²/4: Z = 45 × 12²/4 = 1620 mm³ (the 1.6 Fy S cap does not govern: Z/S = 1.5). φMp = 0.9 × 248 × 1620 = 0.362 kN m; D/C 0.56 (governs).
  5. Equation 6, shear. The shear next to an anchor is the load that has to travel to it from one side: from the span between the anchors half of it, w s/2 = 23.7 kN; from the overhang w a = 16.9 kN. Shear yielding (AISC J4.2a, φ = 1.00): 0.6 Fy h t = 0.6 × 248 × 45 × 12 = 80 kN on the full width. Through the hole see below.

The hole: what the first version of this page missed, and why the plate still passes

  1. The first version put each anchor push at a point on its axis (grey lines in the figure). That gives the largest moment of the overhang, w a²/2 = 0.212 kN m, and the largest shear, 23.7 kN, both exactly at the anchor axis, where the hole is, and it compared them with the full width 45. Through the hole only 45 − 18 = 27 mm are left.
  2. With that model and the net width: φMp,net = 0.9 × 248 × 27 × 12²/4 = 0.217 kN m, D/C 0.98 for bending alone. The shear in the same cut, V/Vp,net = 23.7/48.2, leaves (1 − (V/Vp)²) = 0.76 of the plastic moment (plastic section under moment and shear), so D/C 1.29 on paper.
  3. But the nut cannot push on the hole. It presses on the plate over a ring between the hole (Ø18) and its bearing face (dw = 22, ISO 4032; the washer Ø30 spreads it further, ignored). The blue arrows in the figure. Part of the push now acts on the overhang side of the hole and balances the overhang load right there, so the moment at the axis falls from 0.212 to 0.082 kN m.
  4. Worst cut through the hole with the ring: 37.4 mm from the centre, width left 27.7, M = 0.088 kN m, V = 1.9 kN: capacity φMp,net(1 − (V/Vp)²) = 0.222 kN m; D/C 0.40. Shear rupture through the hole (J4.2b, φ = 0.75): 0.6 Fu (h − dh) t = 58.3 kN against 12.0 kN; D/C 0.21.
  5. Not sensitive to the ring: with the whole push squeezed against the edge of the hole (a ring only 0.5 mm wide), the worst cut through the hole is at D/C 0.44. The cut between the anchors (0.56) governs in every case, and it has no hole.
  6. Summary, type E: bending between the anchors 0.56; bending + shear through the hole 0.40; shear rupture through the hole 0.21. All types: section 7.

4. Side-face blowout towards the top of the pedestal

  1. What it is. A deep head close to a free surface pushes the concrete in front of it outwards, sideways, towards that surface, and a body of concrete between the head and the surface spalls off (red in the figure). It is checked when the head is deep and close to the surface: hef > 2.5 ca1. The nearest free surface of this plate is the top of the pedestal.
  2. The 100. The top of the pedestal is 100 mm above the top of the concrete beams (z = 0): the level where the base plate of the steel column sits. It comes from the site data of the first design (../cantilever_anchor, P.col.top) and is kept in every version; it sets ca1 and the cover over the back plate. Please confirm it on your column drawings.
  3. The back plate (anchor axis) is 71 below the top of the pedestal: ca1 = 100 − 29 = 71. hef = 364 > 2.5 ca1 = 178, so 17.6.4.1 applies.
  4. Size of the spalled body. ACI gives no shape, but its limits imply one: full strength needs 3 ca1 = 213 of concrete to each side of the head (17.6.4.1.1), and two heads stop acting together at 6 ca1 apart (17.6.4.2). So the spall reaches about 3 ca1 from the head on the free surface; drawn so, it is cut by the side faces of the column (ca2 = 165) and the two bodies overlap almost completely (s = 70).
  5. Nsb = 13 ca1 √Abrg λ √f'c = 13 × 71 × √2446 × 1 × 4.54 = 207.1 kN.
  6. Side faces: ca2 = 200 − 35 = 165 < 3 ca1 = 213: × (1 + ca2/ca1)/4 = 0.831.
  7. Group of two, s = 70 < 6 ca1 (17.6.4.2): × (1 + s/(6 ca1)) = 1.164. Nsbg = 200.4 kN.
  8. φNsbg = 0.70 × 200.4 = 140.3 kN against T = 81.3 kN: D/C 0.58

The side faces are free above the beam top (the slab comes later); the top of the pedestal is under the base plate of the steel column. The red body is indicative: the strength comes from the equations, not from the drawing.

6. Why the beam bars are needed, and where the pull goes

  1. Stage 1. The plate presses the concrete in front of it towards the face; tension concentrates at the edges of the plate and the first cracks start there, below the service load (service pull at E about 56 kN).
  2. Stage 2. The crack reaches the nearest free surface first, the top of the pedestal 71 mm above: this is the crack expected in service.
  3. Stage 3. The crack grows down towards the face and crosses the top bars of the beam in line. From then on the bars, not the concrete, hold the cracked body: compression from the plate down to the bars (orange), tension in the bars towards the face (blue). Beam bars: steel D/C 0.76; the crossing must have hook length beyond it: D/C 0.85 with all tolerances against.
  4. Stage 4. The hooks bear against the column ties at the face (contra los estribos). At the ultimate load what gives first is steel: the anchor bar in tension + shear (D/C 0.57) or the beam bars (0.76), both ductile.
  5. After cracking the joint keeps working: the compression path is not cut by the crack, the bars stay elastic in service (about 165 MPa at E), cracks stay thin, and they end up under the base plate grout and the slab. It stops working only if the bars are not where the sheets put them (too low, short hooks, hooks away from the ties).

What is ACI and what is not. The four stages describe how the crack grows (tests on headed anchors: it starts at the head and grows, reaching the nearest free surface first); ACI does not describe a sequence. ACI gives one number, the strength of the complete body, up and down at once: projected area ANc = (71 up to the top + 1.5 h'ef = 165 down) × 400 wide = 94400 mm², with h'ef = 110 because the plate is within 1.5 hef of three edges (17.6.2.1.2, mandatory). φNcbg = 26.3 kN, D/C 3.09. Even ignoring that rule and using hef = 364 with the body cut only by the edges, φNcb ≈ 34 kN, D/C 2.4: plain concrete is not enough either way.

  1. Concrete breakout without reinforcement (17.6.2). The plate is close to three free faces: the top of the pedestal 71 above it and the side faces 165 to each side, all within 1.5 hef, so hef is cut to h'ef = 110 (17.6.2.1.2) and the cone is small: φNcbg = 26.3 kN against T = 81.3 kN (D/C 3.09). A compression from the steel column above would clamp the cone, but ACI does not let it count, and under uplift the top of the pedestal can be in tension. Not relied upon: the pull goes to the beam bars (section 5).

  4. Beam in line (ETABS B6, with the cantilever, + Ev): 46.1 / 72.9 kN m, D/C 0.63.

6c. Stress test: the bars crossing the crack counted as hooked anchors

  1. What changes. Sections 5 and 6 treat the beam bars as reinforcement developed by chapter 25 (bond along the bar plus the hook). Here each bar is treated as a hooked anchor (17.6.3.2.2b): only the bearing inside its hook counts, no bond, eh at most 4.5 db, cracked concrete: φNp = 0.70 × 0.9 f'c eh db = 8.4 kN per Ø12, 11.4 kN per Ø14 tie leg, 5.8 kN per Ø10 tie leg. ACI does not ask for this; it is the most pessimistic reading of the same bars.
  2. Which bars. Only bars parallel to the anchors, within 0.5 hef = 182 of an anchor (anchors 15 mm high), with their hook inside the breakout body (at the cantilever face): the beam top bars (3 / 2 / 3 / 2); the 2 extra beam bars at -80 where they are not in contact with the corner bars (C4 and D4-south); the side legs of the two closed ties Ø14 (at 10 and -24, 127 to 148 from an anchor, held at the face by the tie corner around the corner column bar). 6d. Shear across z = 0: the pedestal block above the beams

  1. What. B1 sits in the top 100 mm of the pedestal, above the beams. The pull T pushes that block towards the cantilever and it must pass T down into the joint across the horizontal plane at z = 0. This is not the 45° diagonal tension of the joint (the joint ties and the joint shear check): it is direct shear transfer along a plane, ACI 318-19 22.9 (shear friction). There is no cold joint there; the plane is monolithic.
  2. Which bars count. Only vertical bars crossing the plane and developed for fy on both sides (318-11 11.6.8; 318-19 22.9). The column bars end in their hooks about 90 mm above the plane (ldh about 205): not developed above, and 25.4.10.2(b) does not allow counting them at a reduced stress. The 4 rods of the steel column are developed both sides (hooks more than 1 m below; base plate and nut above): Avf = 452 mm², φVn = 0.75 (1.4) Avf fy = 200 kN, if the column is mounted and tightened before the cantilever is loaded.

8. Shear anchors A2: shear on the concrete side

Two concrete failures exist in shear, and only one of them is a cone. Pryout (17.7.3) is the tension cone again: the stubby anchor levers about the concrete it bears on at the face, its end nut lifts, and it pulls a cone out behind it, so its strength is kcp Ncb. Shear breakout (17.7.2) is a different body: a half cone pushed out towards the free edge the shear points at. It depends on the distance to that edge, ca1, and hardly on hef (only through le). The first version of the calculation had steel and pryout, and dismissed breakout because V points down where the column goes on. It missed two things: the torsion couple H pushes the shear anchors across the beam, towards a free side face, and ACI also checks a shear that runs parallel to an edge (17.7.2.1(c)). Both are below.

9. Strut-and-tie models of the joint (ACI 318-19 chapter 23)

In short. A strut-and-tie model replaces the concrete of the joint by a truss: struts are concrete in compression, ties are steel in tension, nodes are where they meet. If every part of one truss carries its force (23.3.1: φ × strength ≥ force, φ = 0.75, Table 21.2.1(g)), the joint is safe (a lower-bound result, R23.2.3). R17.5.2.1 allows this method for the anchor reinforcement.

Two trusses are checked, each carrying all of the pull on its own: path 1 sends it into the beam top bars, path 2 sends it diagonally down to the end plate's push on the face. If either one passes, the joint passes. Type E is worked out step by step; the other types are in the table of 9.4.

Starting numbers, type E

  • Pull on the two top anchors: T = 81.3 kN (section 1)
  • Anchors at z = +29; back plate B1: 45 high, 120 wide, its bearing face at u = 364 from the cantilever face
  • Net bearing area of B1 (minus the 2 holes): 4891 mm²
  • Beam top bars (tie of path 1) at z = -68 (the lower layer, conservative)
  • Concrete f'c = 20.59 MPa; bars fy = 420 MPa; anchors fya = 724 MPa
