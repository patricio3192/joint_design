2. Easy check, step by step

Edge beam, which governs. Each step is one idea, one formula, one number. The corner beams are in the table of section 4.

Step 1. Load on the beam

q = 1.2 D + Ev + L = 1.2 (2.5) + 0.579 (2.5) + 2 = 6.45 kN/m²   (1.2D + 1.6L gives 6.20) NEC-SE-DS 3.4.4
Ev = (2/3) I η Z Fa = (2/3)(1)(2.48)(0.25)(1.4) = 0.579 of the dead load. This is the vertical force the NEC asks for cantilevers. The horizontal coefficient 0.3472 g does not load this connection: the slab takes it.
w = q s + beam = 6.45 (4.8) + 0.39 = 31.3 kN/m
At the column face: V = w L = 34.5 kN    M = w L²/2 = 19.0 kN m   (L = 1.1 m from the face)

0.9D − Ev = 0.80 kN/m², still downwards, so the moment never reverses and only the top flange pulls.

Step 2. The moment becomes a pull and a push

The flanges carry the moment as two opposite forces, one flange depth apart: the top flange pulls the column (T), the bottom flange pushes on it (C = T).

T = M / (h − tf) = 19.0 / 0.1915 = 99 kN

The rest of the design is: give T to steel that is anchored on the other side of the column, let C bear on the concrete, and let V go down.

Step 3. Are there enough anchor bars?

φ As fy = 0.75 (4 x 201)(420) = 253 kN ≥ T = 99 kN   D/C = 0.39 ACI 17.5.3, 23.7.2

Would fewer do? 2 Ø16 give 127 kN, D/C 0.78 at the edge. That passes, but with little margin, so the edge keeps 4. The corner beams use 2 (D/C 0.59 with the trapezoid, 0.65 with the envelope).

Step 4. Is each anchor bar long enough? (hook development)

A hooked bar needs a length ℓdh of concrete, measured to the outside of the hook, to reach its yield force without pulling out:

ℓdh = fy ψc db1.5 / (23 √f'c) = 420 (0.796)(16)1.5 / (23 (4.54)) = 205 mm ACI 25.4.3.1, SI (Appendix E)

ψc = f'c/105 + 0.6 = 0.796. The other factors are 1.0 because the hooks are inside the column core and the 4 joint hoops confine them (without the hoops: 328 mm) Table 25.4.3.2, 25.4.3.3.

The bar starts working at the plate, so the first column is the real length. The second ignores the 30 mm along the lug, to be safe. By ACI 318S-14 the need is 249 mm, still within both columns.

Should the 0.90 worry us? No. ℓdh is the length that anchors the bar at its yield force, 84 kN. Each edge bar carries T/4 = 25 kN, 29% of that. ACI does not let the length be reduced for the lower stress (25.4.10.2(d)), so the ratio stays, but the real margin is several times larger. If you want the ratio lower anyway, make the two lower edge bars Ø14: ℓdh = 168 mm, ratio 0.74, and the four bars still give D/C 0.44.

Group effect. ACI has no separate group reduction for development length. Bars close together are covered by ψr: under 6db apart, the length grows by 1.6 unless ties enclose the hooks with Ath ≥ 0.4 Ahs. Here the same ties enclose the anchor hooks and the beam-bar hooks, so they are counted once for all of them: 0.4 (804 + 565) = 548 mm² ≤ 4 ties x 2 legs x 78.5 = 628 mm². The breakout of the group is a separate check: step 5.

Step 5. Who takes the pull after the anchors? (both sides of the breakout surface)

If the anchors were alone, they would pull out a cone of concrete. For these bars chapter 17 gives only 35 kN (D/C 2.8), so the cone must be held by bars that cross it. Those bars are the top bars of the concrete beam in line with the cantilever. ACI accepts this if the crossing bars are strong enough and developed on both sides of the cone ACI 17.5.2.1(a).

5a. Strong enough.

5 Ø12 top bars: φ As fy = 0.75 (5 x 113)(420) = 178 kN ≥ T = 99 kN   D/C = 0.56

So no bars need to be added. Corner: the VCS has 3 Ø12 on top: 107 kN, D/C 0.77; the VCM side is better (D/C 0.46).

5b. Developed inside the cone. The cone starts at the anchor hooks and opens towards the outer face with slope 1 : 1.5. Each beam bar enters it from the beam side and ends in its hook behind the plate. The part of the beam bar inside the cone, from the point where it crosses the cone surface to the outside of its hook, must be at least ℓdh of a Ø12 = 150 mm (ψr = 1.0 thanks to the ties, as in step 4).

Conservative choices: the cone starts at the beginning of the anchor bend, not at the hook, which makes the cone smaller; the crossing point is computed in 3D from the distance between each beam bar and the nearest anchor; the worst beam bar is reported.

Earlier I described this as the two sets of hooks “overlapping”: seen from the side, the anchors and the beam bars run next to each other between their hooks, like a lap. The check above is the precise version of that picture.

The pairs of bars in contact (2-1-2). Two bars in contact form a bundle. For straight development, a two-bar bundle needs no extra length ACI 25.6.1.5; only the cover and spacing conditions use the equivalent diameter √2 db = 17 mm 25.6.1.6, which the beam satisfies. So in the beam nothing changes: ℓd = 529 mm. Hooks are different: ACI does not cover a bundle developed by one hook R25.6.1.5, and advises not to hook bundles as a unit R25.6.1.1. So inside the joint the two bars of each pair should be separated, each with its own hook: the outer bar stays at ±94 and the inner one moves to ±57, with a small crank (1 : 6) starting about 150 mm before the column face. That is where the positions 0, ±57, ±94 come from. It also keeps 8 mm between each beam bar and the anchor tails at ±35.

If the pairs stay together, the geometry still works (length inside the cone 177 mm ≥ 150), but the hooked pair is outside the code. Then count only one bar of each pair plus the centre bar: 3 Ø12 = 107 kN, D/C 0.93. It passes, with less margin, so separating the pairs is the better option.

Does the edge need all 5 Ø12? By strength, 3 bars would do (107 kN, D/C 0.93); all 5 give D/C 0.56. Hook all 5: they cost nothing and are already there.

5c. Developed on the other side (in the beam). The bars continue along the whole beam. They need a straight length of

ℓd = fy db / (2.1 √f'c) = 420 (12)/(2.1 (4.54)) = 529 mm beyond the column face, which a continuous beam bar always has. ACI Table 25.4.2.3

That is the whole check. The beam bars must be hooked down at the outer face of the joint (they have no hooks yet) and must pass between the anchor tails (section 3).

5d. Can the beam carry the moment it receives?

Moment at the column axis: Mface + V (distance face to axis) = 19.0 + 34.5 (0.2) = 25.9 kN m
5 Ø12 top: φMn = 0.9 As fy (d − a/2) = 58.0 kN m   D/C = 0.45 (the cantilever alone) ACI 22.3

Two jobs for the same bars? It is one job. At this column the top bars carry the negative moment of the beam at the face, and the cantilever is part of that moment: it is what makes the support moment negative. The pull of the anchors is that same tension arriving from the other side, not an extra one. What has to be checked is: support moment of the beam from your model without the cantilever + the cantilever moment ≤ φMn. That leaves 32 kN m for the model moment on the VCM (edge) and 16 kN m on a VCS (corner). If your model already includes the cantilever, use its moment alone. The pedestal also takes part of the cantilever moment, so this is conservative.

Step 6. Load paths in easy mode

The moment of the cantilever has to end up somewhere. Two things behind the column can take it, and both are checked:

  • Path 1, the concrete beam: the pull T goes from the anchors to the beam top bars (step 5), and the push C goes straight through the joint into the bottom of the beam. The beam carries the moment like the back span of a concrete cantilever.
  • Path 2, the pedestal: the pull goes diagonally down through the joint to the push. The diagonal compression is carried by the concrete; its vertical part, T tan 31° = 59 kN, is tension in the 3 column bars of the inner face (capacity 190 kN). For this the column bars need their 90° hooks at the top.

Each path alone is enough. In reality the stiffer one, the pedestal, takes most of it.

Pedestal, edge: cantilever 25.9 kN m and frame 21.04 kN m (1.2D + L − Ex), added at right angles as if they happened together: 33.3 kN m against φMn = 100.8 kN m at P = 0, 8 Ø16   D/C = 0.33 ACI 22.4
Joint shear, direction of the cantilever: Vu = T = 99 kN ≤ φVn = 0.75 (1.25) √f'c Aj = 681 kN. Direction of the edge beams, with your frame forces: 105 kN. ACI 15.4.2

Step 7. Shear V

The lug is a small shelf inside the concrete. V pushes it down onto the concrete below it:

φV = 0.65 (1.7 f'c)(width x 2t) = 0.65 (1.7)(20.6)(110 x 24) = 60 kN ≥ V = 34.5   D/C = 0.57 ACI 17.11.2.1

Even without the lug, the welded bars would take it: tension + shear 0.50 ≤ 1.2 ACI 17.8.3.

A 35° plane from the lug to the far face. The shear points down and the pedestal continues below, so there is no free edge for a breakout cone in that direction. Your plane is still a fair conservative check: the wedge above it would have to slide down along it. The plane crosses all 8 column bars. By shear friction:

φVn = 0.75 μ Avf fy = 0.75 (1.4)(8 x 201)(420) = 709 kN, limited to 0.75 (0.2 f'c Ac) = 540 kN. Against 34.5 kN (edge) and 53.3 kN (both corner beams). ACI 22.9.4.2, Table 22.9.4.2, 22.9.4.4

Step 8. Welds: which weld carries what

Three pieces meet behind the plate: the plate, the platina and the bars. One continuous bead joins them, but the forces use its parts differently. There are three forces to follow: the pull T, the push C and the shear V.

8a. Where the pull really acts. The flange pulls the plate at the flange level. The bars pull the plate at their own level. At the edge the two rows of bars sit 14 mm above and below the flange line, so their resultant is on the flange line. At the corner (type C1) both bars sit 14 mm below it. The lever arm to the push C is then shorter, and the bars pull more:

T = M / (zbars − zC)   Edge: 19.0 / 0.1915 = 99.0 kN   Corner: 14.7 / 0.1775 = 82.6 kN (flange force 76.6 kN)

The plate carries the 14 mm offset by bending:

M = Tflange e = 76.6 (14) = 1.07 kN m ≤ φMp = 0.9 Fy b t²/4 = 0.9 (248)(220)(12)²/4 = 1.77 kN m   D/C = 0.61 AISC F11

8b. Which part of the weld carries what.

  • Path A: plate, bead around each bar end, bar. Direct, but the bars sit 14 mm off the flange line, so the plate bends a little.
  • Path B: plate, bead along the platina, platina, side welds, bar. The platina is in line with the flange, so the pull enters it straight; the side welds hand it to the bars.
  • Path C: the shear V. The concrete pushes the platina up between the bars; the bead along the platina takes it to the plate.

In plain words: behind the plate, the pull has two roads to reach the bars, like two ropes tied side by side. Road A is the ring of weld around the end of each bar: the plate pulls the bar directly. Road B goes through the platina: the plate pulls the platina, and the platina pulls the bars through the welds along their sides. Both roads are welded, so both work at the same time. How the pull splits between A and B depends on their stiffness and cannot be known exactly. So each path is checked to carry all of T by itself. Then the split does not matter. For this the side welds were raised from 5 to 6 mm.

Step 9. Torsion on the corner beams

Tt = q (b/4)(unequal width) = 0.36 kN m realistic; 1.33 kN m if all the load sat on one flange tip. Couple on the flanges: 7.0 kN.

At the top, the couple goes to the welded bars as sideways shear (included in the interaction of step 7 at 8% of their strength). At the bottom, it goes by friction under the push C (φ 0.4 C = 25 kN, AISC Design Guide 1). Once the slab hardens, torsion stops.

5. Site

Before the pour
  1. Column bars: 90° hooks at the top, turned into the joint. Mandatory: the pedestal ends in this joint and a straight bar has 410 mm where it needs 705 mm (hooked: 256 mm).
  2. Top bars of the concrete beams: 90° hooks down at the outer face of the joint, tail 12 db = 144 mm. The middle one must step beside the mid-face column bar.
  3. Edge beam (VCM): inside the joint, separate each pair of top bars in contact; the inner bar moves to ±57 with a 1 : 6 crank, the outer stays at ±94. Each bar gets its own hook. The anchor tails at ±35 pass between them.
  4. 4 layers of ties Ø10 in the joint at z = -90 -135 -180 -225 mm, each of 4 straight ties with 135° hooks, and one closed tie at z = +45 after the assemblies.
  5. Lower the assembly (plate, plates and bars, without the beam) from above; the plate closes the form. Fix it to the form and tie it to the cage so it cannot move when vibrating. Corner: beam X first, then Y.
  6. Pour up to the pedestal top (+100) with the rods of the steel column sticking out. Vibrate well under the lug.
  7. Weld the beam on site once the concrete has hardened (7 days is reasonable): fillet 8 all round the flanges, 5 on both sides of the web. Line the top flange up with the marks on the plate, which show where the lug is behind it. Prop the beam tip until the slab has hardened.

Shop: bend the hooks before welding (the welds end 164 mm or more from any bend); bars weldable (INEN 2167 / A706). Keep the platina at the level of the top flange within 3 mm.

6. Notes and references

  • Section numbers checked against the PDFs of ACI 318-19 (inch-pound; SI equations from its Appendix E), ACI 318S-14, AISC 360-16 and NEC-SE-DS.
  • ACI 318S-14 differences: no shear-lug provisions (17.11 is new in 2019); hook length 0.24 fy ψc db/√f'c = 249 mm (25.4.3.1); anchor reinforcement is 17.4.2.9.
  • The excess-steel reduction of development length is not used anywhere: ACI 25.4.10.2(d) forbids it for hooks.
  • Assumed: the VCM top bars in one layer at 0, ±57, ±94 (layout 2-1-2); rebar fy = 420 MPa; I = 1.
  • The frame is an ordinary moment frame (confirmed). The joint ties follow ACI 15.3 (at least two layers within the beam depth, spacing at most 200 mm); the special detailing of ACI 18.8 does not apply.
  • Welds: no directional strength increase (eq. J2-5) is used anywhere, and the combination rule J2.4(c) is not used (it needs one leg size).
  • Revision history. Rev. 1: 4 Ø16 in the pedestal, D = 3 kN/m², L = 1.3 m from the face, 4 anchors per beam, added L bars. Rev. 2: real pedestal (8 Ø16) and beams (VCM, VCS), D = 2.5, L from the axis, short platina, 2 anchors per corner beam, no L bars. Later: 4 tie layers; separated bar pairs in the VCM; beam welded on site; one continuous 8 mm bead joining plate, platina and bar ends; corner anchor force from the real lever arm; no 1.5 factor; side welds 6 mm so that each load path carries all of T.
